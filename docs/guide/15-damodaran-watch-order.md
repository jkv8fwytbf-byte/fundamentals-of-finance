## What it is (plain words and an analogy)

A watch order is a list of his free videos and posts, sorted by what your code needs next. His own syllabus goes intro, then discount rates, then cash flows, then relative valuation. Our order is different, because the engine already exists and you are learning to check it.

Two kinds of material appear below. A webcast is a short recorded lecture, 12 to 20 minutes, hosted on YouTube and listed on his NYU pages. A Data Update is a blog post series he writes every January and February, one post per topic, explaining where his yearly datasets come from.

Analogy: his syllabus is the owner's manual, read cover to cover. This chapter is the index at the back, opened at the page for the part you are holding.

## Why this tool now (for this project)

The calculator copies his spreadsheet, and the nightly jobs copy his datasets. So the only person who can explain why a number is what it is, is him. In Data Update 1 for 2026 he says AI bots "will not only match, but be better than I am, at mechanical and rule-based tasks". He also says he plans to hand almost the whole data update process to his bot. You are building the mechanical part. The judgment part, reading a memo and spotting a story that does not add up, stays with you. Chapter 0, rule 10, calls the corpus the antidote to the tool. This chapter is the dosing schedule.

## How it is used in this project

Each block below maps to one piece of the system. Terms: LTM means last twelve months of financials. EDGAR is the US filings database. A hurdle rate is the return a project must beat, which for us is the cost of capital. A synthetic rating is a credit grade guessed from interest coverage when no agency rating exists. A vintage is the dated copy of a dataset a run used. ERP is the equity risk premium, the extra return demanded for holding stocks over the risk-free rate. CRP is the country risk premium added on top of the mature-market ERP. DCF is discounted cash flow, the method the engine uses.

| Block | What it teaches | Where it lands in the system |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting, sessions 1 to 5 | The three statements, cash versus accrual | Building LTM from EDGAR tags |
| Valuation online, sessions 1 to 8 | Risk-free rate, ERP, betas, cash flows, growth | The input sheet of fcffsimpleginzu, row by row |
| Corporate Finance online, sessions 4 to 11 | Hurdle Rates I to VIII, bottom-up betas, country ERP | The cost-of-capital worksheet and the country-risk vintage |
| Data Update 5 and 4 for 2026 | Provenance of risk and country numbers | Why the India row says Baa3, 1.87%, 7.08%, 2.85% in the January 2026 vintage |
| Corporate Finance online, sessions 12, 13, 17, 18 | Debt, its cost, the cost-of-capital approach | The synthetic rating table; why Apple's missing interest expense is a decision, not a bug |
| Valuation online, sessions 9 and 14 to 18 | Terminal value, multiples | Terminal cost of capital = risk-free rate + mature ERP; the multiples tab |
| The Corporate Life Cycle, 20 sessions | Growth, margin and reinvestment by stage | Memo narratives that are not generic |
| Investment Philosophies | How different investors think | The watchlist framing, and Document D, The Other Side |
| Statistics 101 | Regressions, distributions | Only when you regress across the 94 industry rows |
