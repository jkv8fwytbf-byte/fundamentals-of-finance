# Read one claim carefully

Title, authors/organisation, publication date, link:

## 1. Question

What problem or claim is the source addressing?

## 2. Evidence

What was actually observed, measured or tested? Identify the models/samples,
comparison condition, intervention and outcome when applicable.

## 3. Interpretation

What conclusion do the authors draw from that evidence? Is this a result,
an explanation, a prediction, or a proposed policy?

## 4. Assumptions

Which premises are needed? Which are measured and which are argued for?

## 5. Limits

What populations, models or conditions were not tested? What alternative
explanations remain? What would the evidence not justify saying?

## 6. My calibrated takeaway

In two sentences, state the supported claim and its most important limitation.
Attribute the authors’ position accurately.

## 7. Questions

1. What observation would change my mind?
2. What follow-up experiment or source would reduce my uncertainty?

For MIRI: separate its premises, risk predictions and policy arguments.
For the global-workspace article: distinguish functional access to information
from subjective experience, and inspect what interventions establish.
